Original:

Rogue Artist is a term coined by artist and writer Rafi Perez a few years ago to describe artists who are independent creatives, making a living outside the ‘main stream art system’ of formal post graduate art education, galleries, critics, collectors, organizers and institutions.

Rogue artists are, to be clear, distinctly not ‘outsider artists’ as current culture and the main stream of art label artists who also are social outcasts (those clearly anti-social and or suffering personality disorders).

The key idea is that as a rogue artist you want to do two things: you want to be uniquely creative (true to yourself, not showing unconscious influences of often-in-vogue methods, schools, trends, or even social conventions) and you want to impress (by successfully capturing that creativity in your expression).

But you can’t capture your originality and unique creativity if you don’t know how to fully use and abuse the tools of the trade. It is that simple. This is what Picasso meant by “Pro”.

You impress when your work shows true mastery and command of the ‘tools of the trade’, or the Craft - as well as a conscious ability to be creative with the limits of that craft. You show that the craft and its current conventions do not have command of (or limits on) you, but you have complete command of the tools. This is what Picasso meant by ‘break them like an artist.’

The difference between a true Professional and a craftsperson, or even master craftsman is that both have mastered the craft, but the Pro also understands the ‘why’. And then as an artist, understands when and how and why the rules can successfully be broken.

The true artist, the rogue artist, then works to master craft so that the craft is in service of the Artist’s creativity. And then breaks the rules like a Pro, to achieve the original.

In my case, I have studied and practiced art techniques for thousands of hours, from life drawing to formal architectural rendering, to studying colour theory to mastering the use of colour in various mediums, to learning how to refine my own oil painting mediums and mull my own colours, to understanding how to make, use and manipulate supports and surfaces. The more I learn and master and understand how and why, the more creative I can be with my craft.

Picasso may have meant ‘learn the rules like a Pro’ referring mostly to the formal rules of drawing and composition, colour theory and pictorial design, but the idea encompasses all of the tools of the trade and the craft of employing them.

---

If you understand how something works, then you can change it.

A great many artists in the 1950s, 60s and 70s paid less attention than they should have to basic procedures, such as how to prepare a canvas or what types of paints to use. While breaking artistic and aesthetic rules, they also paid little attention to the “rules” of how to make sure a painting survives. The result is a lot of work for art conservators who struggle to keep millions of dollars of art from falling apart. If you don’t understand a process, it is foolhardy to ignore it.

This advice also referred to the artistic side of things as well. If I truly understand the vibrancy of mixing two particular colors together, or the effect of negative space, or the workings of two-point perspective—to name just three things—then I can play with those concepts. I should hasten to add that this understanding does not have to be academic. Unlike technical aspects of art, the aesthetic understandings can be arrived at in many ways: non-verbal experience, intuition, trial and error, etc.

The point is: learn your stuff so you can be creative!

Art is often about testing boundaries and doing things our own way. If you look at art across history many new movements were an attempt to question the status quo and contemporary (to the time) definitions of art. Impressionism, Realism, Conceptualism, even looking back at the Renaissance and the transition into Baroque considers this.

An artist isn’t bound by any rules, instead s/he/they learn what’s been done before to help inform whats next.
